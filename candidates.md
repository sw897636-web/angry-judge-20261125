# Angry Judge — candidates and pick

Scored against the judge criteria in order: (1) funny rant about an everyday
annoyance, (2) actually funny, (3) specific/concrete, (4) sharp and economical,
(5) freshness. All texts are my own, written for this entry.

## Candidate 1 — The password reset loop (PICKED)

> I forgot my password, so the website made me reset it. My new password cannot be my old password. Fine. I invent a new one, and the site says: "This password was used before." So it remembers my old password perfectly — it just won't let ME use it. The system knows my password better than I do and still treats me like a burglar in my own account.

- (1) rant about an everyday annoyance: yes, pure rant, no essay. 10/10
- (2) actually funny: the paradox punchline ("knows my password better than I do") is a real laugh, not effort. 9/10
- (3) concrete: password reset, exact error messages quoted. 10/10
- (4) economical: every sentence advances the trap; no throat-clearing. 9/10
- (5) freshness: the angle is the system's perfect memory vs. my banned memory — not the usual "passwords are annoying". 8/10

## Candidate 2 — Self-checkout accuses me of theft

> The self-checkout machine just accused me of stealing my own groceries. "Unexpected item in the bagging area." The unexpected item was the bag. I scanned it, I paid for it, and now a robot is yelling at me in front of strangers while a teenager with a keycard strolls over like a parole officer. I came here to buy milk, not to be processed.

- (1) 10/10. (2) funny: "parole officer" and "to be processed" land well. 8/10. (3) concrete: quoted machine message, bag, keycard. 10/10. (4) economical, though "I scanned it, I paid for it" is slightly list-like. 8/10. (5) self-checkout rants are common; the theft-accusation angle helps but is not new. 6/10
- Loses to #1 on freshness and on the punchline: #1's paradox is tighter than #2's scene.

## Candidate 3 — The elevator close-door button is a lie

> The elevator "close door" button is a lie. I have pressed it ten thousand times. The door has never once closed faster. It is a placebo button for adults — a little plastic pacifier they give you so you feel in control while you stand there, trapped in a metal box, listening to music nobody chose, waiting for a machine that has already decided when you may leave.

- (1) 10/10. (2) funny: "plastic pacifier" is a good image, but the laugh is one image stretched over three sentences. 7/10. (3) concrete: the button, the box, the music. 9/10. (4) "listening to music nobody chose" earns its place; the last clause drags slightly. 8/10. (5) freshest angle of the set — placebo button for adults. 9/10
- Loses to #1 on (2): one stretched image vs. #1's escalating trap with a punchline.

## Candidate 4 — "Your call is important to us"

> Your call is important to us. If it were important, you would answer it. Instead I have listened to the same eight seconds of saxophone for forty minutes while a robot tells me my call is important every ninety seconds, like a hostage negotiator who forgot the negotiation. Important calls get answered. Mine got a flute solo.

- (1) 10/10. (2) funny: "hostage negotiator who forgot the negotiation" and "flute solo" are strong. 8/10. (3) concrete: saxophone loop, ninety-second repeats. 9/10. (4) sharp; the repeat of "important" is the joke. 9/10. (5) hold-music rants are well-worn; the negotiator image is the only fresh bit. 6/10
- Loses to #1 on freshness; also "forty minutes" risks sounding like exaggeration rather than shared experience.

## Candidate 5 — The printer

> The printer has one job and it has never done it. It is out of ink, out of paper, or "offline" — offline, while sitting two feet from my laptop, plugged into the wall, doing nothing. I press print, it thinks about it, and then it prints the wrong document from 2019. I did not ask for a time machine. I asked for one page.

- (1) 10/10. (2) funny in places ("time machine") but leans on the familiar. 6/10. (3) concrete: ink, paper, offline, 2019 document. 9/10. (4) economical enough. 8/10. (5) the printer is the single most-ranted-about object alive; the judge has heard it a hundred times. 3/10
- Loses to #1 on (2) and (5): the weakest freshness of the set.

## Candidate 6 — "Are you still watching?"

> I paused a show for two minutes to answer the door, and the TV asked, "Are you still watching?" I was gone for two minutes. The TV sat there, glowing, judging me, as if I had abandoned it. Yes, I am still watching. I never stopped watching. You are the one who stopped believing in me.

- (1) 10/10. (2) mildly funny — the personified TV is cute, not a laugh. 6/10. (3) concrete: two minutes, the prompt. 8/10. (4) economical. 8/10. (5) "are you still watching" is a known meme; the betrayal angle is thin. 5/10
- Loses to #1 on (2) and (5).

## Pick

**Candidate 1, the password reset loop.** It wins on the criteria in order: it is
unambiguously a rant about one everyday annoyance; its punchline is a logical
paradox the reader discovers with the narrator (the system remembers the
password perfectly and still bans me from using it), which reads as a real
laugh rather than performed anger; it is maximally concrete (quoted error
messages, exact sequence); every sentence tightens the trap; and the angle —
the machine's perfect memory vs. my forbidden memory — is fresher than the
printer, the bus, or hold music. Final text is in main.ts (revised once more
in-session after the first draft, per the transcript gate).
